# Series Concept

*Stage 1 story bible. Source of truth for world, tone and series engine.*

## Logline

At Camp Baskerville, every creak, squelch and spooky sound gets blamed on the legendary
Hound, so kid detective Holmes and best friend Watson look closer, notice more and think it
through, proving every time that the "monster" is something much friendlier.

## Short pitch

It's summer, and campers have come to an old stone manor at the edge of a big wild park.
Around the campfire, the camp storyteller tells the legend of a giant shaggy hound with
glowing eyes who howls across the park whenever a Baskerville comes home. This year, a
Baskerville *does* come home, and strange things start to happen.

Holmes, the camp's self-appointed detective, and Watson, the cabin-mate who keeps the case
journal, follow the clues. In the pilot they find the truth: the "Hound" is a big, gentle,
very lost dog. He becomes the camp's secret mascot. But at a camp full of imaginations,
whenever anything odd happens, someone always whispers, "It's the Hound!" And Holmes always
clears his name.

## Adaptation principles

What we keep from Doyle:

- A mysterious family legend
- A spooky moor (now a big, friendly-wild park)
- A new arrival at a big old house (Henry Baskerville)
- Strange clues: footprints, a lost boot, a light in the window, a howl in the night
- A detective who solves it with observation and logic, plus a loyal friend who writes it down

What we change:

- **Everyone is a child.** Doyle's adult roles become campers, helpers and club kids.
- **No curse, no death, no danger.** The legend is a campfire tale told with a wink.
- **The hound is a friendly dog.** He is misunderstood, never menacing.
- **The "villain" is a kid with a harmless motive** who learns a lesson and is welcomed back.
- **Watson's narration becomes a case journal** full of doodles.

## The world

### Camp Baskerville

A summer camp in an old stone manor everyone calls **the Hall**. It was the Baskerville
family home long ago. Now it has bunk rooms, a big dining hall with a long wooden table, a
kitchen full of clattering pots, a creaky grand staircase, a portrait gallery of
Baskervilles (including some very silly old hats), and an attic that nobody is *quite* sure
about.

Around the Hall: cabins (mixed, named for plants: Heather, Bracken and Gorse), a campfire
circle, a dock on a small pond, a craft barn and a flagpole. The Hall's front steps have a
wide wooden ramp that the campers painted, and inside is a slow, star-painted cage lift that
reaches the upper floors and the attic.

### Baskerville Park (the moor)

A big, wild, beautiful park at the edge of camp. Rolling hills of purple heather, winding
trails and wide open sky.

| Landmark | Description | Story use |
|---|---|---|
| **Tor Hill** | A tall lookout rock with a view of the whole park | Spying, signals, telescopes, sunsets |
| **The Stone Circle** | Old ruins of standing stones, mossy and mysterious | Secret meeting spot, echoes, hide-and-seek |
| **The Grimpen Squelch** | A harmless, ankle-deep mud flat that goes *squelch* | Lost boots, muddy footprints, slapstick |
| **Heather hills and trails** | Purple hills, paths and hollows | Chases, tracking, nature finds |
| **The Squelch Lookout Deck** | A round wooden platform at the end of the Boardwalk beside the Grimpen Squelch, built by campers, with a daisy railing, flag, pulley with rope and bucket, and a bell ("Squelch Control") | A high viewing spot; Watson's favourite place |
| **The East Terrace** | A flat stone balcony on the Hall's east side with a thick railing, a rooster weathervane on the tower above, and an old brass chimney pipe | Wind, weather, a safe high view over the heather |
| **The Boardwalk Trail** | A wooden path through the heather from the Hall to the Stone Circle and the foot of Tor Hill, with a flower painted on every post | Accessible route for everyone; Mateo's chair rolls it easily |

⚠️ The Grimpen Squelch is **never dangerous**. Nobody sinks. The worst that happens is a
stuck boot and a funny noise.

### Dusk and fog

At dusk, soft **glittery mist** rolls in over the heather. It glows gold and pink in the
setting sun. It makes the park feel magical and a little mysterious, never dark or
threatening.

## The legend

Told each summer at the first campfire by the camp storyteller (Mortimer). About **one
minute** long, told with a twinkle.

> *Long, long ago, when the Hall was new, the Baskervilles had a hound.
> A great big hound. A shaggy hound. A hound as tall as a pony,
> with eyes that glowed like two little moons.
> And whenever a Baskerville came home to the Hall...
> the hound would climb to the top of Tor Hill
> and howl across the park:
> "Woo-oo-oooo!"
> Some say he's still out there.
> Some say he's waiting.
> And some say... he just wants a snack.*

The last line always gets a laugh. There is **no curse** and **no harm** in the legend.
It's a fun, shivery story, and the storyteller winks at the end.

## Series engine

1. **Pilot:** a full, kid-sized adaptation of Doyle's novel. A Baskerville arrives, the
   Hound seems to return, and Holmes and Watson uncover the truth: the Hound is a big,
   friendly, lost dog, and the spooky happenings were set up by the Nature Club president to
   keep campers away from a rare butterfly before the Park Prize.
2. **After the pilot:** the dog becomes the camp's **secret mascot**. Only a few kids know
   he's real and where he naps.
3. **Every episode:** something spooky or strange happens at camp. Someone whispers, "It's
   the Hound!" Holmes and Watson investigate and **clear the Hound's name**, finding the
   real, harmless explanation.

Why it works for kids:

- A repeatable hook they can anticipate and shout along with
- A lovable dog they want to protect
- Mysteries kids can solve along with Holmes, with every clue shown on screen
- A world with room for many small adventures: kitchens, attics, ponds, hills, ruins

## Tone

- **Warm:** friends look out for each other. Nobody is mean for long.
- **Funny:** slapstick in the Squelch, silly misunderstandings, a dog who loves snacks.
- **Adventurous:** exploring the park, night walks with lanterns, secret signals.
- **Mild suspense:** the "ooh, what was that?" kind, always resolved with a smile.
- **Language:** simple, rhythmic and fun to read aloud. Short sentences. Repeated phrases.

## Content rules

Never in the show:

- Violence, fighting or hurting anyone (including the dog)
- Death, curses or serious illness
- Weapons of any kind
- Peril that feels real (falling, drowning, sinking, being lost alone at night)
- Mean-spirited bullying that goes unresolved
- Stereotypes. Diversity shows through names, looks, family details, food and traditions,
  and every character has a real personality, strengths and flaws.

## Scare flags

These rules keep spooky fun on the right side of scary.

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Fog and dusk** | Always golden-hour and sparkly. Never black, never pitch-dark. |
| **The howl** | A silly "woo-oo-oo," more harmonica than horror. |
| **Glowing eyes** | Always a reflective collar or glow paint, revealed as harmless. |
| **The Hound's look** | Shaggy, goofy, oversized and floppy. Shown in silhouette only before the reveal. Never shows teeth or growls. |
| **Night scenes** | Lanterns, flashlights, fireflies and stars keep scenes lit and cozy. |
| **Being alone** | Kids explore in pairs or groups. A trusted friend is always close by. |
| **Jump moments** | At most one small "boo!" per episode, followed straight away by a laugh. |
| **Mystery load** | At most 3 clues and 1 red herring per episode. |

When a scene might be too scary or too complex for ages 5-8, the script marks it with
`⚠️ FLAG` and a suggested fix.

## Visual style

- Stylized 3D with a **storybook look**: soft, rounded shapes, painterly textures,
  warm light. Not photoreal.
- Palette: heather purples, moss greens, stone greys, golden sunsets, lantern ambers.
- Clue moments are marked by a **sparkle** and a **soft chime** (see
  `episode-formula.md`).

## Grown-ups

Camp Baskerville is run by kids, in storybook logic. Adults exist only as **off-screen
voices, letters, photos and notes** (Nana Ngozi's letters, Henry's video calls, Grandpa
Barrymore and Bà Ngoại calling from the kitchen, a note from "the Camp Chief").
