# Episode Formula

*Stage 1 story bible. Every episode follows this shape so kids know what's coming and can
solve along.*

## The rules of an episode

- About **11 minutes**
- One **self-contained mystery**, watchable in any order
- One small **lesson**: kindness, honesty, teamwork or curiosity
- At most **3 clues** and **1 red herring**
- Every clue is **shown twice** before the reveal, so kids can spot it themselves
- The mystery always has a **harmless explanation**, and the Hound is always cleared

## Beat sheet

| Time | Beat | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00-1:00** | Cold open | Something spooky-funny happens at camp. A camper whispers, "It's the Hound!" |
| **1:00-4:00** | The case arrives | Watson narrates from the case journal. Someone brings Holmes the problem. Holmes observes the scene. **Clue #1.** |
| **4:00-8:00** | Investigation | Into Baskerville Park. One funny **red herring** sends them the wrong way. **Clues #2 and #3.** All three clues are seen a second time. |
| **8:00-9:30** | Reveal and resolution | Holmes puts it together. The mystery-maker has a harmless motive. A kind resolution. The lesson is said simply. |
| **9:30-11:00** | "How Holmes Figured It Out" | Recap. Each clue replays with a glowing outline, and Holmes explains how it fit. |

### Beat notes

**Cold open (0:00-1:00)**
- Short and punchy. A sound, a shape in the mist, a missing thing.
- End on the whisper: *"It's the Hound!"* Kids will learn to say it along.
- Spooky tension is broken with a joke before the theme song.

**The case arrives (1:00-4:00)**
- Watson opens the journal: *"Case journal, day ___. Something strange happened at camp..."*
- Holmes studies the scene and notices something no one else did. That's **Clue #1**.
- Holmes's method chant appears for the first time.

**Investigation (4:00-8:00)**
- Out into the park: Tor Hill, the Stone Circle, the Grimpen Squelch or the heather trails.
- **Red herring:** a funny wrong lead (for example, the "glowing eyes" are a cat on a
  fence). It should make kids laugh, not confuse them.
- **Clues #2 and #3** are found.
- By 8:00 every clue has been seen **twice** (once when found, once again in a new light,
  in Watson's journal or a callback).

**Reveal and resolution (8:00-9:30)**
- Holmes gathers everyone and connects the clues in order.
- The mystery-maker explains why. The motive is always harmless and relatable (wanting to
  win, feeling left out, protecting something, being embarrassed).
- Nobody is shamed. The group helps put things right.
- The lesson is said once, simply, by a kid in their own words.
- The Hound is cleared. Optional: a quick wink from the secret mascot.

**"How Holmes Figured It Out" (9:30-11:00)**
- Holmes and Watson look back through the journal.
- Each clue replays with a **glowing outline** and a short line from Holmes:
  *"Clue one: the muddy boot. Mud from the Squelch is grey, not brown. So..."*
- Ends with the method chant and a final laugh.

## Kid-detective devices

### Clue Alert
A **soft chime** and a **sparkle** whenever a clue appears on screen. Kids learn: chime
means "pay attention!"

> **Animator note:** the sparkle is small and warm, a gentle twinkle on the object. It
> appears both times the clue is shown.

### Holmes's method chant
> **"Look closer. Notice more. Think it through."**

Said by Holmes (and, over time, other kids) when it's time to solve. Shown with a **golden
magnifier frame** that circles the scene. Used at least once in the investigation and once
in the recap.

### Watson's case journal
Watson writes and **doodles** every clue. The journal:
- Opens and closes the narration
- Gives a second look at clues (the "shown twice" rule)
- Holds the recap pages in "How Holmes Figured It Out"

> **Animator note:** doodles are hand-drawn 2D in crayon and pencil style, sitting on the
> 3D page.

### "It's the Hound!"
The cold-open whisper. A recurring catchphrase for the whole camp, not one character.

## Clue design checklist

A good clue for ages 5-8:

- [ ] Can be **seen** on screen (an object, mark, colour, sound or smell shown visually)
- [ ] Is **concrete**: a muddy boot, a feather, a cookie crumb, a glowing smudge
- [ ] Needs only **one simple step** of logic ("grey mud means the Squelch")
- [ ] Is shown **twice** before the reveal, with the Clue Alert both times
- [ ] Points toward the answer without giving it away by itself

## Red herring checklist

- [ ] Is **funny**, not frustrating
- [ ] Is cleared up **on screen** before the reveal
- [ ] Doesn't need to be remembered to understand the solution

## Lesson checklist

- [ ] One lesson per episode: **kindness, honesty, teamwork or curiosity**
- [ ] Shown through action first, said in words once
- [ ] Never preachy. A kid says it, in kid words.
