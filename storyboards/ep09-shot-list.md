## Checks against the shot-language guide

- [x] The shadow-hound is soft-edged, gold-brown and floppy; it copies Tavita within seconds of
  appearing, so it reads as a game, not a threat
- [x] The sun is always low, warm and golden; the noon drawing is the daytime contrast
- [x] Hair is never the joke: Holmes's cap flaps (not her loc buns) form the hound's pointed ears
- [x] Henry's swinging arm is the tail; his gangly build is played with affection
- [x] Every clue shows twice with matching framing (table above)
- [x] The chime is reserved for clues; the sheep's \"baa\" and the soft harp are not clues
- [x] Sign close-up (\"always wandering\") holds hands and face with a subtitle
- [x] Moony appears only after the case is solved (scared of his own shadow)

## Notes and questions for the producer

1. **Padding used:** the timing report said ep. 9 was the shortest, about 2:44 under. The extra time
   goes into the shadow-play scenes: the kids waving and wiggling at the shadow-hound while it
   copies them (scene 4), the four-part \"it's us\" reveal (scene 5), and the shadow-animals scene
   (scene 6, now about 90 seconds), plus the Moony-and-his-shadow button.
2. **Shadow staging:** the key is that kids at home can try the game themselves. Keep the
   shadow-hound visibly synced to the kids' movements, with one clear \"copy\" per second.
3. **Clue 3's cut-through** (scene 5) shows four quick shots, one per shadow part, with only one chime.
4. **The cap flaps** need to be clearly visible tied up on top of Holmes's cap in every scene
   (the pointed-ear silhouette depends on them).
5. **Duchess's round shadow** gag must read as a cloud on legs, not a creature.
