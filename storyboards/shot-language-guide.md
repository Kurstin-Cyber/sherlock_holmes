# Shot Language Guide

*Stage 5 · Approved with producer notes · Applies to every episode*

This is the series' visual grammar. If a shot list and this guide disagree, change one of them
on purpose and tell the producer.

---

## 1. Frame, pace and feel

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Frame** | 16:9 widescreen |
| **Look** | Stylized 3D, storybook: soft rounded shapes, painterly textures, warm light |
| **Animation feel** | Animate characters "on twos" (a new pose every other frame) for a hand-made, storybook feel; camera and effects run smooth |
| **Average shot length** | 5 to 7 seconds. Longest 14 seconds, only for emotional peaks or establishing views |
| **Cutting rhythm** | Cut on action or on a line. Never more than three static shots in a row |
| **Transitions** | Mostly straight cuts. Use a **page-turn wipe** into and out of Watson's journal, a **golden iris** out of Holmes's magnifier, and a **dissolve** only for time passing |
| **Breathing room** | After every clue and every emotional beat, hold one extra second. Children aged 5 to 8 need time to see it |

---

## 2. Camera height and angles

The default is a camera at the kids' own eye level, which makes the world feel big and the
gang feel close. It is a **default, not a firm rule**: use wider, lower or higher views when
the story calls for wonder, a map-like view of the park, or a joke. The respectful framing
rules below still apply.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Default camera height is about a ten-year-old's eye level.** Adults are never in the frame, so the world is kid-sized and grand. Vary it when the story asks. | The audience feels they are one of the gang |
| **Mateo Watson is always shot at eye level or slightly below, never from above.** No shot may look down on him, or tilt up at an adult looking down at him. In two-shots, the camera sits at his eye line, with standing characters leaning in or crouching to meet it. | Respect, and equal footing |
| **Henry Baskerville is shot with a gentle low angle** to show he is tall, softened with warm light and a bobble hat, never looming. | He reads as lanky and friendly, not big and threatening |
| **Zaid Selden is framed small in a big, warm space.** Wide shots with plenty of sky and friendly shapes around him; never from above. | Smallness feels safe, not vulnerable |
| **Moony is shot at ground level or at kid height, with a goofy detail in frame** (floppy ear, wagging tail, sneeze, sock). Before the reveal he appears only in silhouette (see section 4). | He reads as a big puppy |
| **No dutch (tilted) angles** in scenes with spooky sounds. A level horizon means "safe." | Tilted frames signal danger |
| **No extreme close-ups of eyes** except through Holmes's magnifier. | Avoids scary staring shots |
| **Wide shots for wonder, close shots for feeling.** The park, the Stone Circle and the sunset are always wide. Henry's photo, Zaid's hug and Beryl's blush are close. | Clear emotional grammar |

### Screen direction

- Out along the Boardwalk, away from the Hall, the team moves **left to right**.
- Returning to the Hall, they move **right to left**.
- Holmes is usually frame-left, Watson centre or frame-right, Henry behind them. This keeps the
  trio readable at a glance.

## 3. Light and colour

| Time of day | Look |
|---|---|
| **Morning** | Fresh, pale gold, long soft shadows, dew sparkle |
| **Midday** | Bright, clean, short shadows (used for contrast in the shadow episode) |
| **Golden hour / dusk** | Rose, apricot and violet sky. Glittery mist at ankle height. Warm rim light on everyone. This is the show's signature look. |
| **Night** | Never black. Every night shot has at least two of: lantern pool, fairy lights, moon night-light, moonlight, fireflies, stars. The darkest area on screen is still readable, a deep blue-violet, never black. |
| **Indoors** | Warm lamps and fairy lights; wooden floors; sunbeams with dust motes by day |

**Rule of thumb:** if a frozen frame still looks cosy as a greeting-card image, it is safe.

## 4. Moony's silhouette rule (before the reveal)

In the pilot, Moony appears only as a silhouette until Zaid's den. Every silhouette shot must:

1. Show **a floppy ear and an upright ear**, plus **a wagging tail**.
2. Be **backlit gold or rose**, never black on black.
3. Be followed within **three seconds** by a funny action (a sneeze, a head tilt, a sit).
4. Cut away to a **friendly face** (Holmes's grin, Zaid's wave) within **five seconds**.
5. Never show teeth, never growl, never charge or stalk. He bounds.
6. Be at a **distance** (wide shot), never rushing toward camera.

## 5. The Clue Alert shot (every clue, twice)

This is the show's most important shot. Kids learn to watch for it.

### First showing

| Beat | Shot | Dur |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reveal the object** in the scene, framed so the clue is clearly readable (CU or MS). Hold. | 1.5 s |
| 2 | **Slow push-in** begins. **Chime** (soft, two notes, bell-like) and a **warm sparkle** on the object. | 1.5 s |
| 3 | A **golden outline draws on** around the clue (hand-drawn feel, 1 second to complete). | 1.0 s |
| 4 | Cut to **Holmes's reaction** (a head tilt, "Curious!") or Watson reaching for the journal. | 1.5 s |
| 5 | **[JOURNAL]** page-turn wipe to the **doodle** with a gold star, then wipe back. | 3.0 s |

### Second showing (the replay)

Use **exactly the same framing and camera angle as the first showing** so children recognise
it. The outline is already glowing when it appears, the chime plays, and a narrator line (Holmes
V.O. in the recap) names it. The match of framing is the "memory" device.

### Rules

- The sparkle is **small and warm**, not a flash. No more than **three twinkles per second** and
  no full-screen flash (photosensitivity).
- Every sound has a **visual equivalent**: the sparkle for the chime, ripples for noises (below).
- The chime is **never** used for anything but clues.

## 6. Other recurring shots

### The chant: "Look closer. Notice more. Think it through."

- A **golden magnifier frame** (a round brass rim with a gentle glow) swings in from the edge of
  the screen and **circles** the thing being studied. 2 seconds.
- Holmes says the line looking at the object, not the camera.
- When the whole team says it (episode 4), the frame **splits into six small rims**, one around
  each speaker, then merges.

### Watson's journal

- **2D crayon and pencil doodles on a 3D page.** The page curls into frame with a soft
  paper-turn sound.
- Doodles **draw themselves** in 1.5 seconds; the gold star pops last.
- The journal never covers a speaking face. It sits in the lower third of frame.

### Sound you can see (ripples)

| Sound | Visual |
|---|---|
| **Howl or "woo"** | Soft golden musical notes drifting out of frame |
| **Wind, hum** | Pale blue wavy lines |
| **Creak, vibration** | A ripple of gold light along the wood |
| **Quack, small animal sounds** | Yellow ripples |
| **Whimper** | A small pale-blue ripple |
| **Paper flap** | Paper-coloured ripples |

### Smells you can see (ribbons)

- A **coloured ribbon** with tiny **icons inside** drifting up from the source: lilac with paint
  pots, golden with biscuits, green-gold with flatbread and herbs.
- Holmes sniffs; the ribbon appears; the Clue Alert follows.

### The paper-puppet legend

- Switches to **paper-cut puppets** on sticks against a glowing backdrop, with
  **Anjali's shadow-puppet hands** visible in a corner of frame, to tell children "this is a
  story."
- Sharp edges, visible paper texture and visible sticks; flat colours.
- Returns to 3D with a **page-turn** and Anjali's wink.

### The "How Holmes Figured It Out" recap

- Always set in **Heather Cabin at night**: fairy lights, the wall chart, Holmes and Watson on
  the floor, the journal in the centre.
- Each clue is a **three-step**: (1) journal doodle glows; (2) **cut to the original shot**,
  replayed with the glowing outline; (3) cut back to Holmes, who says the line.
- The last shot is Watson writing "Case closed. Hound cleared." with **three bell dings**.

## 7. Sign language, subtitles and hearing aids

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Signing is framed so both hands and the face are visible.** Medium shot or tighter, never cropped at the wrist. | Signing needs hands and expression |
| **Subtitles** float in a soft white speech-shaped bubble just below the signer's hands, never over faces. | Readable and not intrusive |
| **Close-up on signs that teach** ("yes," "sorry," "help"), held 2 seconds. | A tiny gift to young viewers |
| **Minh's hearing aids** are bright orange and catch the light. They are never shown being "fixed" or hidden. | Ordinary and proud |
| **Captions** are provided for all dialogue; **all sounds** have a visual equivalent (section 6). | Accessibility |

## 8. Sound and music cues

*Notes for the sound designer and composer.*

| Cue | Direction |
|---|---|
| **The howl** | A harmonica with a wobble at the end. Round, soft, silly. Never low, never long (max 3 seconds). |
| **Title sting** | 8 seconds of harmonica over the magnifier swirl. The same wobble is the pilot's Clue 2. |
| **Clue chime** | Two soft bell notes, bright, reserved for clues |
| **Watson's bell** | One ding nervous, two ding happy, three dings solved |
| **Dynamics** | No sudden loud sounds. Peak level stays within a gentle range; the cold-open howl is the loudest scary-ish sound and it is soft. |
| **The "boo" moment** | At most one per episode, followed within two seconds by a laugh |
| **Silence** | Use two seconds of near-silence before emotional beats (a hug, a reveal) |
| **Music** | Warm acoustic: harmonica, ukulele, fiddle, soft percussion, a small string section for emotion. No horror drones. |

## 9. Scare-safe checklist for every shot list

Before a shot list is approved, tick every item.

- [ ] No frame is darker than a deep blue-violet, and every night scene has two light sources
- [ ] No dutch angles in spooky moments
- [ ] No sudden zooms toward a creature
- [ ] The Hound is seen only as a silhouette with a floppy ear and a wagging tail until the reveal
- [ ] Every spooky sound has a funny cut within ten seconds
- [ ] Kids are always in a group or a pair when something spooky happens
- [ ] The camera never looks down on Watson
- [ ] Every clue shows twice with matching framing
- [ ] No flashes brighter than a sparkle, no more than three twinkles per second
- [ ] Every sound has a visual equivalent
- [ ] Signing has hands and face in frame, with subtitles

---

## 10. Producer decisions

1. **Animating "on twos"** for a storybook feel: approved.
2. **Camera at kid eye level:** a default, not a firm rule (see section 2).
3. **Matching framing for clue replays:** approved.
4. **Transitions** (page-turn wipe, golden iris, paper-puppet switch) and the **six-rim team
   chant** (episode 4 onwards): no changes requested; kept as written.
